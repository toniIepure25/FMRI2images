
\chapter{Software Design, Implementation, and Validation}
\label{chap:implementation}

\section{Analysis and Design Goals}

The scientific results of this thesis are only meaningful if a strict chain of alignment is preserved end-to-end: between NSD trials, the predicted CLIP target, the cached supervision tensor, the trained checkpoint, the SHARED1000 evaluation gallery, and the qualitative export pipelines. A single off-by-one in any of these stages can silently produce numerically plausible but scientifically meaningless results, so the supporting software is treated here as a research instrument whose validity is part of the contribution. Four requirements follow directly: strict sample/target/identifier alignment; rapid experimental iteration without silent protocol drift; comparability between model versions under a common retrieval-metric suite and the same SHARED1000 gallery; and full traceability of every reported number to a concrete YAML configuration, checkpoint, and metrics-export artifact. To satisfy these requirements the pipeline is decomposed into loosely coupled stages --- data preparation, target caching, model training, retrieval and fusion evaluation, result aggregation, and qualitative export --- so that a regression can be localised to a specific stage instead of treating the whole system as a black box.

\section{Software Architecture and Implementation Flow}

The implemented system follows a staged architecture: a \textbf{data layer} (NSD-derived features, identifiers, splits), a \textbf{target layer} (CLIP-based latent targets and cached variants), a \textbf{model layer} (baseline and multi-head decoders with their losses), an \textbf{evaluation layer} (retrieval metrics, shortlist reranking, fusion scores), and an \textbf{analysis layer} (experiment manifests, qualitative outputs, and leaderboard summaries). At run time, NSD samples are loaded under a split-aware preprocessing regime; targets are extracted on demand or read from caches; the decoder is trained with the loss appropriate to its experiment family; validation-time retrieval is computed on a fixed gallery with CSLS correction and optional shortlist reranking; and results are aggregated into machine- and human-readable summaries comparable across runs. Abstracting this flow as a deterministic map
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}:(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}) \longmapsto \mathcal{R},
\label{eq:pipeline_map}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}$ is the data and split definition, $\mathcal{C}$ the configuration, $\mathcal{A}$ the cached artifacts and checkpoints, and $\mathcal{R}$ the resulting metrics and qualitative outputs, clarifies what must remain fixed when two experiments are compared.

Versioning matters because the experimental narrative depends on comparing many iterations, not on a single final run. Every major experiment family is associated with an explicit YAML configuration that records data sources, target choices, architecture hyperparameters, loss weights, training settings, and evaluation options. This makes it possible to attribute a score change to a modelling idea rather than to a silent protocol drift.

\section{Validation and Testing Strategy}

Testing in this project takes several complementary forms: \textbf{split-integrity checks} that prevent repeated images from leaking across train and validation sets; \textbf{shape and alignment checks} that verify every prediction matrix matches its ground-truth target matrix; \textbf{metric sanity checks} on controlled inputs; \textbf{checkpoint and cache verification} during evaluation; and \textbf{qualitative inspection} for failure modes that aggregate metrics can hide. Several improvements in the final system did not come from inventing a new loss function but from identifying and eliminating hidden sources of inconsistency; rigorous validation acted as a performance multiplier rather than as an after-the-fact check.
