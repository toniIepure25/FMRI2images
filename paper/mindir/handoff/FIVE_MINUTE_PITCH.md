# Five-minute pitch (talk track)

*No hype. Development cohort N = 8. Independent replication is future work.*

## 0:00–0:45 — The problem
When we imagine a scene, visual cortex activity resembles perception — but not identically. Decoding imagery is
held back by how much imagery data you must collect per person. The open question: which parts of the imagined
neural state does perception fail to specify, do those parts look the same across people, and how little direct
imagery data do you need to recover them?

## 0:45–1:30 — Data and approach
I used the public Natural Scenes Dataset — eight participants, a discovery cohort — and a fully pre-registered,
frozen analysis pipeline. The key move is to split the imagined state into a part perception can supply and a
residual it cannot, then ask whether that residual is shared across people and how cheaply it can be calibrated.
The participant is the unit of inference throughout; I never count trials or schedules as subjects.

## 1:30–3:15 — Findings
First: perception is informative but incomplete — a real, low-dimensional component of the imagined state lies
outside the perceptual representation. Second: that component is participant-specific under the tested analysis
— it does not transfer across people and is not predicted by perception, anatomy, or behaviour. Third: a small
direct calibration recovers much of it — eight observations, four imagined identities measured twice, was the
smallest burden I tested that recovered at least half the gap in both visual streams. I want to be clear that is
not a universal minimum; it is specific to my estimator and cohort. Fourth: the frontier is fragile — which
measurements you acquire matters, and the repeat-to-repeat geometry of that outside-of-perception component
predicts whether a calibration will generalise, in all eight participants.

## 3:15–4:00 — Limitations
Eight people, one cohort, analysed repeatedly; the results depend on the chosen representation and estimator; no
causal claim; and no independent replication yet. Exploratory follow-up shows the failure-predictive geometry is
structured but distributed — not a single latent variable.

## 4:00–5:00 — Replication and the ask
To test generality, I have prospectively frozen and preregistered an independent replication — planned twelve
participants, minimum eight, with three preregistered secondary geometric endpoints — but it has not been run. I
am looking for scientific feedback and, if the design holds up, a supervisor and MRI access to run it. I am not
asking for a commitment beyond a read of the work.
