# Novelty-claim certification (P3)

All positioning verified against primary sources (`literature_primary_source_audit.csv`). The manuscript makes
a **combination-of-contributions** claim, not a priority ("first") claim.

## What the manuscript must NOT claim (verified precedents)
- **Perception/imagery transformations are new** — **NO.** Directly preceded by **Roy et al. 2025** (bioRxiv
  2025.09.02.672180): a vision→imagery transformation that predicts imagery activity, halving/reorienting
  dimensions in early visual cortex. MINDIR extends this framing.
- **Generalizable neural transformation operators are new** — **NO.** **Ward, Isik & Chun 2018** (J Neurosci
  38:8526) show transformation functions that generalize to novel objects under affine perceptual change.
- **Cross-subject common spaces are new** — **NO.** **Haxby et al. 2011** (Neuron 72:404) hyperalignment and
  **Chen et al. 2015** (NeurIPS) SRM precede this.
- **Perception-trained decoding of imagery is new** — **NO.** Established, and **MIRAGE (Kneeland et al., PLOS
  Comput Biol 2026)** explicitly addresses vision→imagery decoding generalization on NSD-Imagery.

## Certified combination-of-contributions sentence (approved for Introduction/Discussion)
> Building on transformation-based analyses of perception and imagery, we jointly characterize identity-held-out
> perception-to-imagery mapping, test its transfer across participants, decompose target imagery geometry into
> perception-supported and target-specific components, and prospectively quantify the amount and acquisition
> robustness of the direct participant-specific imagery calibration required to recover the missing geometry.

## Certified short-form sentence (approved for Abstract/Introduction; no priority claim)
> Rather than asking only whether perception predicts imagery, we ask which components of imagery geometry
> perception fails to specify, how those components vary across participants, and how much direct imagery data
> are required to recover them.

## Differentiation statements (approved)
- **vs Roy et al.:** Roy establishes the imagery-transformation framework and reports systematic vision→imagery
  transformations; MINDIR independently reproduces/extends parts of it and adds identity-held-out
  generalization, cross-subject transfer, shared-vs-subject-specific decomposition, the perception-support
  ceiling, the direct target-state calibration burden, acquisition-schedule robustness, and repeat-geometry
  predictors. We do **not** imply Roy should have tested these.
- **vs MIRAGE:** MIRAGE performs brain→image reconstruction/decoding across vision and imagery; MINDIR studies
  brain-state→brain-state representational transformation and calibration geometry — related but distinct target
  spaces and questions.
- **vs hyperalignment/SRM:** these establish methods for shared information across idiosyncratic topographies;
  MINDIR uses such shared-space concepts as tools and asks whether a perception→imagery transformation itself
  transfers to an imagery-unseen participant and which target-state components remain subject-specific.
- **vs Ward 2018:** Ward shows held-out-object generalization of neural transformations across affine
  perceptual changes; MINDIR differs in the state transition (perception→imagery), the cross-participant
  transfer question, the perception-support decomposition, the calibration frontier, and acquisition robustness.

## Verdict
**Novelty is defensible as a combination of contributions on a development cohort, with a preregistered
independent replication.** No "first/novel/unique" priority claim is warranted or made.
