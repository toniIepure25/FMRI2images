## Frank Scharnowski

**To:** Prof. Frank Scharnowski

**Subject:** MSc project — perception vs mental-imagery neural geometry (feedback / possible supervision)

Dear Prof. Scharnowski,

I am an MSc Cognitive Science student working on how the brain represents seen versus imagined images, and I would value your view on a short piece of work.

Using the public Natural Scenes Dataset, I found that perception carries substantial but incomplete information about the imagined ('target') neural state; the missing component is low-dimensional but participant-specific under my analysis, and can be calibrated from a small amount of direct imagery data — though that calibration is fragile to which measurements are acquired. This is a **development cohort of eight participants**, which I treat as such: I have prospectively frozen and preregistered an **independent replication**, but it has **not** been run.

Your group's work on real-time fMRI and analysis methodology is the closest methodological fit I have found in Vienna: the core open question is exactly an acquisition/analysis one — how much imagery data, and which, are needed to calibrate to a person's imagined state.

I am writing to ask whether you would be willing to give scientific feedback and, if the design holds up, to discuss supervising or hosting the independent replication. I have attached a two-page brief and the manuscript; a one-page replication plan is available on request. I am not asking for any commitment beyond a short conversation.

Thank you for considering this.

Best regards,
Antoniu Iepure
[MSc Cognitive Science; institution; email]
