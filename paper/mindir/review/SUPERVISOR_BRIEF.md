# Supervisor brief — MINDIR discovery manuscript (read in < 5 min)

## What we found
In N = 8 (NSD, discovery cohort), perception explains a lot of imagery structure but not all: a **low-
dimensional, subject-specific target-state residual** sits outside perception support. It does **not** transfer
across people and is **not** predicted by perception/anatomy/behaviour. A small direct imagery calibration — **8
observations (4 identities × 2 repeats)** — recovers ≥ half the native-oracle gap on average in both visual
streams, but the frontier is **not schedule-robust**, and **repeat-to-repeat outside-support geometry (Q_OUT)**
predicts whether a calibration generalises (ρ 0.42 / 0.48; 8/8).

## Why it matters
It reframes imagery decoding as a **calibration/data-cost** problem with a **subject-specific** target geometry
and a **geometric quality predictor** — practically useful (how much imagery data, and which repeats, you need)
and conceptually distinct from treating imagery as perception replay.

## What is solid (prospectively frozen, primary)
- Outside-support residual exists and is low-dimensional (O2.5/O2.6).
- Subject-specificity under the tested representation (O2.10/O2.11/O2.13).
- 8-observation frontier under the frozen estimator (O2.9/O2.12).
- Not schedule-robust; Q_OUT predicts robustness (O2.14/O2.15).

## What is exploratory (same cohort, follow-up)

  anisotropy but the dominant mode does **not** predict failure. Net: failure geometry is structured but
  **distributed** — a constraint, not a mechanism.

## Biggest weakness
**N = 8, single discovery cohort, repeatedly analysed; the "8-observation" and "subject-specific" results are
estimator/representation-conditional.** No independent replication yet.
