**Subject:** Feedback request — imagery calibration geometry (MSc project, discovery-cohort manuscript)

Dear [Prof./Dr. Surname],

I am an MSc Cognitive Science student working on human visual mental imagery, and I would value your view on a
short manuscript I have prepared.

Using the public Natural Scenes Dataset, I studied how much of the imagined ("target") neural state perception
can and cannot account for, and how little direct imagery data are needed to calibrate to the part it cannot.
Briefly: perception is informative but incomplete; the missing component is low-dimensional but
participant-specific under the tested analysis; a small direct imagery calibration recovers much of it (eight
observations was the smallest burden I tested that worked on average — not a universal minimum); and that
calibration is fragile to which measurements are acquired, with repeat-to-repeat geometry predicting success.

This is a **discovery/development cohort of eight participants**, and I am careful to treat it as such. To test
whether the findings generalise, I have **prospectively frozen and preregistered an independent replication**,
but it has **not been run** — no independent participant has been scanned.

I would be grateful for: (1) your honest scientific feedback on the manuscript and its claims; and, if you think
it worthwhile, (2) a conversation about whether the independent replication is worth pursuing, and what access
or supervision that would realistically require. I am not asking for any commitment beyond a read.

I have attached a two-page brief and the manuscript, and can send a one-page replication plan on request.

Thank you for considering this.
