# Reviewer A — Systems / Computational Neuroscience

Scope: conceptual novelty, neural interpretation, relation to imagery/perception literature, whether the
mathematical constructs carry neuroscientific meaning, whether subject-specificity is overinterpreted, and
whether the work advances understanding beyond Roy et al. Severity tags: CRITICAL / MAJOR / MODERATE / MINOR.

## Summary judgement
The manuscript is careful and unusually well-controlled, and the central story (perception is informative but
incomplete; the residual is low-dimensional but subject-specific; minimal calibration is possible but fragile)
is coherent. My concerns are about **interpretive reach** and **conceptual delta over prior work**, not about
the honesty of the analyses.

## Major concerns
- **[MAJOR] Neuroscientific meaning of the constructs.** "Target-state geometry", "outside-support residual",
  "drift mode", and "anisotropy" are estimator/representation quantities. The manuscript mostly respects this,
  but a systems reader will ask what, if anything, these map onto neurally. Recommend an explicit paragraph
  stating that these are properties of the decoding representation, not identified neural mechanisms, and that
  no cortical-source, layer, or process claim is made.
- **[MAJOR] Conceptual delta over Roy et al. and the transformation view.** The paper must state crisply what
  is genuinely new relative to (a) the perception→imagery transformation framing it extends, and (b) shared-
  response/hyperalignment work on cross-subject geometry. My read: the *new* contributions are the
  outside-support residual decomposition, the minimal-calibration frontier with its schedule fragility, and
  the repeat-geometry predictor — not the transformation concept itself. This should be said in one sentence.
- **[MODERATE] Subject-specificity risks overinterpretation.** "Subject-specific" is supported only as *failure
  of cross-subject transfer and failure of prediction from tested covariates* at N=8. It should never read as
  "each brain has a private imagery code." Consistently qualify as "under the tested representation and
  transfer procedure."

## Minor concerns
- **[MINOR]** The ventral/lateral agreement is reassuring but two ROIs is thin; parietal is reserved — say why
  (it is a secondary, not a primary stream) so it does not look like selective reporting.
- **[MINOR]** The X-series is interesting but should not read as the paper's centre of gravity; the abstract
  currently gives it appropriate but nearly equal billing — keep it clearly secondary.

## What would cause rejection
- Any implied causal/mechanistic or "neural drift proven" claim (currently avoided — keep it that way).
- Framing eight observations as a general sufficiency result for imagery decoding.
- Presenting X1–X4 as confirmatory evidence rather than exploratory follow-up on the same cohort.

## What would change my confidence
- A single, defensible novelty sentence that survives a literature check.
- Explicit "these are representation-level quantities, not neural mechanisms" framing.
- Clear, repeated development-cohort + pending-replication signposting.
