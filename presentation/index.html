Predict Meaning, Not Pixels

Map brain activity into CLIP's semantic embedding space where cosine similarity encodes semantic similarity.

Formal mapping

\(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i), \quad f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{15724} \to \mathbb{R}^{768}\)

nsdgeneral ROI voxels → ViT-L/14 CLIP embedding (L2-normalized)

If the predicted embedding lands near the true one,
nearest-neighbour retrieval identifies the image.

[60 sec] "The core insight: don't reconstruct pixels — predict MEANING. OpenAI's CLIP model maps images into a 768-dimensional unit hypersphere where cosine similarity equals semantic similarity. Our decoder maps ~15,700 brain voxels from the nsdgeneral cortical region into this space. If the predicted embedding lands near the true embedding, nearest-neighbour retrieval identifies the exact image from the gallery." System Architecture: Triple Expert Fusion

[50 sec] "The architecture, left to right: 7T fMRI from NSD subject 01, masked to 15,724 nsdgeneral voxels, per-session z-scored. Three independently trained decoder experts: — V61a: maps to 197K-dimensional token space, strongest alone at 83.1%. — V62a: maps to 768-D CLS space, weaker at 47.5% but geometrically different. — V66a: maps to 768-D with ROI pretraining, 39.1% alone. Their CSLS-corrected scores are z-normalized and combined with fixed weights 0.7/0.1/0.2. Evaluated on SHARED1000: 1000 images never seen during training or validation." TRANSITION: "Let me explain the core insight — why three experts beat one."

Core Contribution: Frozen Score Fusion

Thesis claim: A frozen weighted combination of hubness-corrected, z-score-normalised retrieval scores from three independent experts yields a statistically stronger ranking than any single expert.

\(s_{\text{final}}(i,j) \;=\; 0.7\,\tilde{s}_{\text{V61a}} \;+\; 0.1\,\tilde{s}_{\text{V62a}} \;+\; 0.2\,\tilde{s}_{\text{V66a}}\)

where \(\tilde{s}_e = (s_e^{\text{CSLS}} - \mu_e)/\sigma_e\)  |  CSLS at k = 3  |  weights frozen before SHARED1000 evaluation

V61a (w=0.7) — 197K token-space, MC-TTA-16
Dominant ranker • 83.1% alone

V62a (w=0.1) & V66a (w=0.2)
Complementary score geometry • different failure modes

[70 sec — intellectual core of the talk] "This is the main contribution. Three experts, each independently trained, each producing CSLS similarity scores against the full gallery. At inference: CSLS hubness correction at k=3, then z-normalize per expert so scale differences don't dominate, then fixed weighted sum. Weights tuned on validation, FROZEN before test. No data leakage. No test-time tuning. V61a dominates at 0.7 — it's the strongest single model. But V62a and V66a, despite being weak alone, contribute geometrically distinct score signals." Why it works

The experts differ in target dimensionality, training objective, and inductive bias — they fail on different queries.

The R@5 rise (96.9% → 98.0%) is consistent with complementary correction rather than redundant ensembling.

[60 sec] "Why does this work? The three experts are fundamentally different: V61a targets a 197K-dimensional token space with 16-draw Monte Carlo test-time augmentation. V62a uses a 768-D CLS target. V66a uses ROI-pretrained features. They're trained with different objectives and map to different geometries. The key insight from the thesis: they fail on DIFFERENT queries. Evidence: R@5 goes from 96.9% to 98.0%. If experts were redundant, they'd reinforce the same top-1 mistakes. Instead, we see the tail of slow-decaying ranks shrinking — consistent with complementary correction, not redundant ensembling. The gain is modest (+3.2pp) but meaningful: it's a held-out benchmark with no tuning." TRANSITION: "Let me show specific examples of what the experts retrieve."

Mathematical Formulations

vMF-NCE (training loss for V61a)

\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{vMF-NCE}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \frac{\exp\bigl(\kappa_i \cos(\hat{\mu}_i, z_i)\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp\bigl(\kappa_i \cos(\hat{\mu}_i, z_j)\bigr)}\)

CSLS hubness correction (Conneau et al., 2018)

\(\text{CSLS}(x,y) = 2\cos(x,y) - r_T(x) - r_S(y)\)

\(r_T(x) = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{y' \in \mathcal{N}_k(x)}\cos(x,y')\) — mean similarity to k nearest neighbours; penalises hub vectors

Frozen triple fusion (inference only)

\(s_{\text{final}}(i,j) = \sum_{e \in \{61a, 62a, 66a\}} w_e \cdot \frac{s_e^{\text{CSLS}}(i,j) - \mu_e}{\sigma_e}\)
