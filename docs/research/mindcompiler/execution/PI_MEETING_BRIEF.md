# PI meeting brief (<=2 pages)

## 1. 30-second summary
How much of what we imagine can be read from what perception already encodes, how much is specific to the
individual, and how little imagery data is needed to calibrate to it? On 8 participants (public data) I find a
real, low-dimensional but subject-specific imagery component and a small, fragile calibration frontier. I want to
replicate this in an independent cohort.

## 2. 3-minute scientific story
Perception and imagery share visual cortex but not identical geometry. Splitting the imagined state into a
perception-supported part and a residual, a substantial low-dimensional residual sits outside perception. Its
orientation does not transfer across people and is not predicted by the tested perception/anatomy/behaviour, yet
a few direct imagery measurements recover much of it — but success depends on which measurements are acquired
(repeat-to-repeat geometry predicts it). Exploratory analyses show the failure-predictive structure is
distributed, not a single latent variable.

## 3. Established findings (development cohort, N=8)
Perception incomplete; low-dimensional subject-specific residual; ~8-observation calibration frontier under the
frozen estimator (not a universal minimum); schedule-fragile; repeat-geometry predicts calibration success.

## 4. Main limitations
N=8 development cohort analysed repeatedly; results conditional on the estimator/representation; no independent
replication yet; no causal/mechanistic claim.

## 5. Frozen replication
An independent replication (planned N=12, min 8) is prospectively frozen and preregistered, with three secondary
geometric endpoints; the acquisition, analysis, and outcome-firewall are all fixed in advance.

## 6. Future value (M1/M2/M3)
Online "enough-data" stopping; zero/near-zero-shot prediction of a person's imagery geometry; and cross-state
generalization (e.g. delayed recall). Each is preregistered and requires new data.

## 7. What I need from the PI
Scientific feedback; a decision on supervision/hosting; guidance on site (3T vs 7T), acquisition changes before
ethics, and the ethics pathway.

## 8. What I can already provide
A frozen analysis pipeline, engineering-ready acquisition software (simulation-tested), a site-integration and
authorization package, and an externally reproducible codebase.

## 9. Five questions to ask (see PI_QUESTIONS.md for the full list)
Framing strength? Least convincing claim? Support a replication? 3T vs 7T? N≈12 realistic operationally?

## 10. Likely objections + concise answers
- *N=8 is small* — agreed; it is a development cohort; that is exactly why replication is preregistered.
- *Is it just reliability/noise?* — a scalar reliability variable did not predict calibration; the predictor is
  geometric (and will be re-tested prospectively).
- *Is it representation-dependent?* — likely partly; a cross-representation robustness test is planned.
- *Mind reading?* — no; this is representational geometry, not content decoding, and no causal claim is made.
