Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, **"Subject-specific geometry and calibration limits in human visual imagery,"** for
consideration at [Journal].

**Scientific question.** How much of the human perception→imagery transformation is shared across people, how
much is subject-specific, and what limits minimal calibration of a decoder to the imagined ("target") neural
state?

**What we did and found.** In a **discovery/development cohort of N = 8** participants from the public Natural
Scenes Dataset, using a prospectively frozen analysis pipeline, we show that perception carries significant but
incomplete information about target-state geometry; the missing component is low-dimensional (under the frozen
representation) yet **subject-specific under the tested representation and transfer procedure** — it does not
transfer across participants and is not predictable from the tested perception phenotype, anatomy, or behaviour.
A direct calibration of **eight imagery observations** (the smallest prospectively tested common burden under
our frozen estimator, not a universal minimum) recovers at least half of the native-oracle gap on average in
both visual streams, but this frontier is **not schedule-robust**: repeat-to-repeat outside-support geometric
agreement predicts whether a calibration generalises. Exploratory analyses indicate the failure-predictive
repeat variation is structured but distributed — not a discrete latent state, a scalar reliability variable, or
a single dominant drift mode.

**Why it fits [Journal].** [The minimal-calibration frontier, its acquisition fragility, and the
reproducibility engineering / the representational-geometry framing] are of direct interest to your readership.

**Status and rigor.** These are **discovery-cohort** results; we make no causal or mechanistic claim. Every
reported value traces to a sealed, hashed artifact, and the full pipeline is prospectively frozen and
server-verified. A genuinely independent replication is **already preregistered and frozen** (with three
preregistered secondary geometric endpoints) but **not yet executed**; we present the current work as
prospective, testable hypotheses for that replication, not as established generality.

**Reproducibility.** All analyses run from committed source with per-gate configuration hashes, provenance
manifests, and tests; the Natural Scenes Dataset is public and no raw data are redistributed.

We confirm the manuscript is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and that all authors approve
submission.
